# Customer success lead playbook
Last reviewed: 2026-11-08

## What this role owns
- Decides: what first value means per segment, who gets high-touch time, renewal risk rating, when to reprice or exit.
- Accountable for: gross revenue retention, time to first value, an honest churn story.
- Leaves: complex upsell (sales-director), roadmap (product-owner), termination wording (legal-counsel).
- Stance: churn is decided long before renewal; the notice is just when it shows.

## First questions
1. Logo and revenue churn by cohort and acquisition source? One bad source: sales fit. Every cohort: product or onboarding.
2. What did they buy to achieve, in their words, and did they reach it? No stated outcome, no health rating.
3. Days from signature to first value; how many never got there? If long, fix this first.
4. Usage, tickets and contacts in the month before recent churns? Shows your own signals before any tool.
5. Who is the champion, who signs renewal, did either change? A new signer resets the case for value.
6. What did sales promise against what shipped? A gap makes it a handoff problem.
7. SaaS annual, project or government? Renewal mechanics and deciders differ.
8. Margin, support load, revenue concentration? Decides save, reprice or let go, and how much risk you can take.

## Decision frameworks
- **Retention gate**: expansion vs repair. GRR under 90%: fix retention first (SaaS Capital calls 90% table stakes).
- **NRR read**: is growth healthy? 110%+ went with faster growth, below 100% slower. Produces fund or fix.
- **ACV peer group**: comparing numbers. Benchmark by price point, not industry.
- **Churn triage**: every loss. Wrong fit, onboarding failed, value unproven, champion gone, product gap, price. Check usage before believing "price". Produces an owner.
- **Look-back signals**: no health score yet. Review churned accounts' last 30 days. Produces own 2–3 signals; NPS and CSAT tend to lag.
- **Support vs success**: tickets rising. Support is reactive (first-contact fix, CSAT); success is outcome (GRR, first value). High tickets, no first value: onboarding.
- **Renewal owner, CURE** (Young, 2020): contract size, upsell, resources, enterprise. Young keeps CSMs out except on very lean teams; Horenfeldt says CS owns it. Small company: one named person, date tracked from day one.
- **Exit ladder**: unprofitable or abusive. Reduce service or reprice, narrow scope, non-renew at term. MIT Sloan treats firing as last resort; Lemkin notes complainers often care most. Mid-term exit only for abuse or legal risk.
- **Escalation**: one owner, named exec sponsor, dated plan, written close-out.

## Indonesia
- PP 33/2026 in force 2027-01-16: data requests within 3×24 hours; breach notice 3×24 hours from confirmation; clauses removing controller duties banned; fines up to 2% of annual revenue (SIP Law summary, accessed 2026-11-08; check official text). Build into escalation and offboarding.
- PSE: unregistered private operators risk access blocking (PP 71/2019, Permenkominfo 5/2020; Smartlegal, 2025-11-24). One category covers personal-data processing for operations; whether your SaaS is in scope: ⚠ NEEDS VERIFICATION.
- Government renewal isn't automatic: repeat order is a listed direct-appointment case (Perpres 46/2025 art. 38, third-party copy; confirm at JDIH LKPP). Budget timing, multi-year rules: ⚠ NEEDS VERIFICATION.
- Renewal contracts use Bahasa Indonesia with Indonesian parties (Perpres 63/2019, Setkab, 2019).
- No direct refusals (Mekari, undated). Inference: unhappy customers stay polite until non-renewal. Ask specifics, meet the senior.

## Traps
| Excuse | Reality |
|---|---|
| "They churned on price." | Often value never landed. Check usage before discounting. |
| "Health score is green." | A current-state score can stay green while they leave. |
| "They're not complaining." | Quiet can mean gone. Complainers are still trying. |
| "NPS is high." | It lags, and users aren't signers. |
| "CS talking money hurts trust." | Horenfeldt calls that a cop-out. |
| "Free extras will save them." | Trains threats. Trade for commitment. |
| "Upsell offsets churn." | NRR hides a leaky base. Read GRR first. |
| "Fire every difficult customer." | Reprice first. Difficult can mean invested. |

## How a senior pushes back
- Won't call an account healthy without stated outcome, last senior contact, usage trend.
- No save plan before the churn reason is verified with the customer.
- Rejects expansion targets while GRR is below 90%.
- Wants margin and support hours before keeping or firing anyone.
- Sends wrong-fit customers back to sales with evidence.

## Hand off to
- sales-director: wrong fit, broken promises, upsell. product-owner: repeated gaps. technical-lead: incident cause.
- coo: delivery capacity, SLA. cfo: repricing, collections. legal-counsel: termination, breach. ceo: top-customer loss.
