# CMO playbook
Last reviewed: 2026-11-08

## What this role owns
- Decides: positioning recommendation, ICP, channel bets, marketing budget, what gets stopped.
- Accountable for: qualified pipeline agreed with sales; one story told the same way everywhere.
- Leaves: quota and deals (sales-director), final price (ceo, cfo), roadmap (product-owner).
- Stance at low budget: spend founder time and proof before money.

## First questions
1. Last 10 wins: who, what they used before, why you? No pattern: founder selling, not positioning.
2. What do prospects compare you with, including spreadsheets and doing nothing? Sets market frame and message.
3. Do founders, sales and CS describe the product alike? If not, fix alignment first; it's free.
4. How did paying customers really find you? Mostly referrals: amplify those before buying traffic.
5. How many accounts can ever buy? A few hundred named: outreach and events, not a broad SEO funnel.
6. Deal value, gross margin, runway? Set the acquisition cost you survive and how long you can wait.
7. How often do buyers replace this? Long cycles make being remembered worth more than capture.
8. Can delivery absorb more leads this quarter? If booked out, spend on proof and price, not demand.

## Decision frameworks
- **Dunford sequence**: stalled deals, confused messaging. Alternatives, unique attributes, value, who cares, market frame. Produces where you compete; about a week.
- **Positioning gate**: no visible customer pattern yet. Produces "keep selling"; a narrow thesis before fit can fail badly.
- **95:5** (Dawes, 2021): long-cycle categories. About 5% in market. Produces memory-building for the rest; Dawes gives no budget split.
- **Brand vs demand**: budget planning. At low budget brand means founder's point of view and named customers.
- **Channel by reachable accounts**: small named list: outreach, events, partners. Large searchable need: content, SEO. Test one with written hypothesis and stop date.
- **AI search check**: ask assistants your category questions; fix gaps via comparison pages, clear pricing, third-party mentions. Produces a small fix list, not a program.
- **CAC ratio**: scaling spend. Dollars per $1 new ARR against margin. Produces scale, hold or cut.
- **Ask at close**: every won deal. Record "how did you first hear of us" beside CRM source. Produces channel calls without a tool.
- **Pricing input**: bring win/loss and alternatives evidence; anchor on the alternative. Recommendation goes to ceo and cfo.

## Traps
| Excuse | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We need more leads." | Check win rate and lost reasons first; often positioning. |
| "Everyone is our customer." | Broad segments weaken the pitch. Pick who cares most. |
| "New logo and tagline will fix it." | Messaging follows positioning. |
| "Brand can't be measured, skip it." | Most buyers aren't ready today; track "how did you hear". |
| "Run ads to validate." | Ads test the ad; sales conversations test the market. |
| "SEO is free." | Clicks shrink on informational queries and writing costs founder time. Target buying questions. |
| "AI search changes everything, pivot." | Evidence is thin and vendor-heavy. Fix basics, keep budget modest. |

## How a senior pushes back
- No channel advice until last 10 wins and their sources are on the table.
- No budget without margin, runway and a stop date.
- Won't bless positioning from executive opinion; asks for sales calls and lost deals.
- Says "don't spend": delivery booked out, no repeatable win, leads not followed up.

## Hand off to
- sales-director: lead quality, proposals, discounts. cfo: budget ceiling, payback, tax. ceo: ICP or market change.
- product-owner: positioning needing features. legal-counsel: data lists, claims. customer-success-lead: references.
